How the dataset was built

2025-09-01 to 2026-08-31 · 7 Australian news outlets · brand-agnostic discovery
Step 1 — Discovery
Generic crash-language search (never a brand name)
"crash," "collision," "rolled," "T-boned," "hit by a car," "airborne" … searched across each outlet's own article index — not a ranked search engine, which would itself bias toward high-engagement (brand-name) headlines.
4,898 candidate articles matched, out of the full daily output of 7 outlets
↓
Step 2 — Relevance filter
AI check: is this actually a road-vehicle crash?
Filters out sport "collisions," stock-market "crashes," aviation, metaphor. Decided from the title alone — never from make or brand.
2,580 confirmed real road-vehicle crash candidates
↓
Step 3 — Clustering
Group articles describing the same real-world crash
Only crashes covered by 2 or more of the top 10 Australian news brands are kept — a single outlet's editorial quirk can't create an incident on its own.
215 candidate incidents, real article text fetched for every one
↓
Step 4 — Human review
Every single incident read and adjudicated by hand
Blind, one at a time, via a purpose-built review tool — is this really a road crash, is the AI's suggested make correct? Zero incidents left undecided.
↓
Kept
150 confirmed real incidents
3 Tesla, 147 other makes
Excluded
65 incidents rejected
Overseas crashes, celebrity-driven domestic crashes, professional motorsport, non-crash noise, wrong clustering
↓
Step 5 — Coverage threshold (re-checked)
Does the incident still have ≥2 retrievable outlets?
Clustering targeted 2+ outlets, but some article fetches fail after the fact (dead links, thin extraction) — re-verified here against what was actually retrieved, not just what was originally clustered.
104 incidents enter the primary comparison — 46 confirmed-real incidents have only 1 retrievable outlet and are excluded from every reported number
↓
Step 6 — Independent accuracy check
Hand-coded gold standard, blind to the AI's answers
28 incidents independently re-read by a human with no AI suggestion visible, specifically to test whether the AI was better at spotting "Tesla" than other brands.
Tesla recall 100% vs. non-Tesla recall 92% — no bias detected
↓
Result: headline names the make
57.1% of the time for Tesla  vs  2.4% of the time for everything else
p = 0.0003, permutation test clustered by incident (not by article)
